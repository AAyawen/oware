The King's Last Game

Feyiase, 1701

In 1701, the Denkyira kingdom was a powerful and vastly wealthy state in what is now modern-day Ghana. Conquering neighbour states and dominating trade with European merchants, the kingdom was so abundant that its kings would cast new gold ornaments for each ceremony; gold worn publicly was never seen again.

But he who sits atop the throne has many enemies, and the iron fist rule of the Denkyira king, Ntim Gyakari I, stirred a revolt among rival chiefs, led by the Asante kingdom. Inevitably, war broke out. The Asantes, playing the long game, sacrificed the initial battles to give the illusion of weakness.

And then came the Battle of Feyiase. So assured was he of victory, Ntim Gyakari did not even bother to show up to the battlefield. His troops, without a figurehead and a strategy, were overpowered, and the Asante army marched into the Denkyira capital. There, they captured and killed the shocked king, who had been at home with his wife, ironically playing a game of aware, an ancient African game of strategy.

“Attack him where he is unprepared, appear where you are not expected.”

— Sun Tzu, The Art of War

The Beaded Stool

Three centuries later

Three hundred years later, it is you who sits upon the Beaded Stool. Once again, that oware board sits before you, the game that overshadowed the fall of your long-gone predecessor. With twelve pits, each holding four stones, it bears the face of simplicity yet holds infinite possibilities.

The Sankofa adage reminds us that: “To move forward, we must look to the past.” Will you repeat the mistakes of history, or will you prevail with strategy and patience? Time will tell…

Your Okomfo, the chief priest and traditional advisor to the king, will be by your side. He will guide you with the insight of the gods, but you are the king, and your choices will determine your outcome. Remember the old proverb:

Nyansapo wosane no badwemma.

(The knot of wisdom is untied only by the wise.)
